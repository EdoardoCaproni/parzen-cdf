\begin{abstract}
We estimate the cumulative distribution function (CDF) of a known distribution from samples, using a
\textbf{Parzen Window} estimator, and we read the density off as the derivative. Working from a
\emph{known} distribution lets us score every estimate against the exact truth. This first part builds
the estimator on the simplest case --- a single Gaussian --- one ingredient at a time: we choose the
\textbf{window size}, verify the fit, and study how the estimate behaves with the number of samples.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup and notation}

We draw samples $x_1,\dots,x_n$ from a known distribution and estimate its CDF $F(x)=\Pr[X\le x]$ and
its density $f=F'$. A \textbf{Parzen Window} estimator places one smooth window on each sample and
averages them. We use a logistic window, chosen so that the \emph{integral} of the window is the
logistic sigmoid $\sigma$; the estimated CDF is then the integral of the estimated density, available
in closed form:
\begin{equation}
  \fhat(x)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n \frac1h\,\sigma'\!\Big(\frac{x-x_i}{h}\Big),
  \qquad
  \Fhat(x)=\int_{-\infty}^x \fhat
          =\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n \sigma\!\Big(\frac{x-x_i}{h}\Big).
  \label{eq:parzen}
\end{equation}
The parameter $h>0$ is the \textbf{window size}: small $h$ gives a sharp, noisy estimate, large $h$ a
smooth, blurry one. We score an estimate by the \textbf{CDF gap} --- the Kolmogorov--Smirnov distance
$\max_x|F(x)-\Fhat(x)|$, the largest vertical gap between the estimated and true CDF (smaller is
better) --- and we also track the recovered density's peak height as a finer check.

\section{The single Gaussian $N(0,1)$}

\subsection{Choosing the window size}

We start as simply as possible ($n=2000$) and add one ingredient at a time, each motivated by the
previous shortcoming (Table~\ref{tab:window}, Figure~\ref{fig:window}).

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \caption{Window-size strategies on $N(0,1)$, $n=2000$.}
  \label{tab:window}
  \begin{tabular}{llc}
    \toprule
    Strategy & Window size & CDF gap \\
    \midrule
    fixed, trivial value      & $1.0$            & $0.157$ \\
    adaptive (per-point), pilot $=1.0$ & mean $1.0$ & $0.151$ \\
    \textbf{Silverman (data-driven)}   & $0.196$    & $\mathbf{0.020}$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A fixed window of size $1.0$ over-smooths.} The estimated density is far too wide and
  low (peak $0.21$ vs the true $0.40$): the logistic window of ``size one'' is itself broad. CDF gap
  $0.157$.
  \item \textbf{Making the window adaptive does not rescue a too-large pilot.} Per-point window sizes
  built from the fixed pilot barely shrink in the dense centre and only widen in the tails (they came
  out at mean $1.0$, range $0.93$--$1.91$), so the over-smoothing remains. CDF gap $0.151$.
  \item \textbf{A data-driven global window size (Silverman) solves it.} Window size $0.196$ brings
  the gap to $0.020$; estimate and truth coincide. This is our estimator for the single Gaussian.
\end{itemize}

  \caption{Window-size strategies. The fixed and adaptive windows over-smooth (red, orange); the
  data-driven Silverman window (green) matches the truth (black) for both density and CDF.}

\subsection{How many samples do we need?}

We sweep the sample count (Silverman window, averaged over $5$ seeds; Table~\ref{tab:n},
Figures~\ref{fig:nsweep}--\ref{fig:nprog}). Two things stand out.

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \caption{Effect of the sample count on $N(0,1)$ (Silverman window, mean over $5$ seeds).}
  \label{tab:n}
  \begin{tabular}{rccc}
    \toprule
    $n$ & window size & CDF gap & density peak (true $0.399$) \\
    \midrule
    50     & 0.397 & 0.066 & 0.324 \\
    100    & 0.332 & 0.058 & 0.361 \\
    200    & 0.312 & 0.051 & 0.349 \\
    500    & 0.256 & 0.034 & 0.364 \\
    2000   & 0.197 & 0.019 & 0.368 \\
    20000  & 0.124 & 0.008 & 0.390 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The CDF is easy, even with very few samples.} At $n=50$ the CDF gap is already only
  $\approx 0.066$, and by $n=500$ it is $0.034$. The reason is structural: the CDF is the
  \emph{integral} of the density (Eq.~\ref{eq:parzen}), and integration averages out the
  finite-sample noise --- so the CDF estimate is far more forgiving than the density.
  \item \textbf{The density (peak) is the demanding quantity.} It is what needs samples: the peak
  climbs from $0.32$ ($n=50$) to $0.39$ ($n=20000$), closing the gap to the true $0.399$ only with
  abundant data. Convergence is slow, the expected Silverman rate (window size $\propto n^{-1/5}$).
\end{itemize}

\section{Summary of Part I}

On the single Gaussian, a Parzen Window with a data-driven \textbf{Silverman window size} estimates
the CDF essentially exactly (gap $\approx 0.02$ at $n=2000$, $\approx 0.008$ at $n=20000$). The CDF is
robust even at tiny sample counts because it is an integral; the density's peak is the quantity that
demands data. The next part carries this estimator to harder targets --- mixtures of Gaussians.
